Privacy Policy

Retainer · last updated 29 October 2026

Retainer is a private journal organised around the things you do. This policy explains, in plain terms, what the app stores, where it goes, and who can see it.

The short version. Your check-ins, notes and photos are stored on your iPhone and in your own iCloud account. They are not sent to me, and I cannot read them. There is no advertising, no analytics and no tracking of any kind.

Who this policy is from

Retainer is made by Tom Walton, contactable at retainerapp@icloud.com. In data-protection terms, there is very little for me to be responsible for. Everything the app stores lives in your own Apple account. I operate no servers, hold no copy of your journal, and have no technical means of reading it.

Where your data lives

Retainer stores your information in two places:

  • On your iPhone, in the app’s own database. It is encrypted by iOS and unreadable while your phone is locked and switched off.
  • In your iCloud account, using Apple’s CloudKit service, so your data syncs between your devices and reaches anyone you choose to share with.

This matters: your journal is held in your iCloud storage, under your Apple account. I do not operate servers that hold it, and I have no technical means of reading it. Apple processes it on your behalf under Apple’s privacy policy.

What the app stores

  • Actions — the names, emojis and descriptions of the things you track.
  • Check-ins — the date and time you recorded something, plus any note or photo you attached.
  • Photos you add to a check-in.
  • Location, if you allow it — see below.
  • Reminder settings, which stay on your device.
  • A nickname and an optional photo, so people recognise you on actions you share with them. Both are optional and can be skipped.

Location

Retainer can save your location when you check in, so you can remember where something happened and see your check-ins on a map. This is optional: the app asks your permission before using your location at all, and you can turn it off at any time in the app (Settings → Location → Save my location when I check in) or in iOS Settings.

When it’s on, the app saves your coordinates as you check in and turns them into a readable location — for example a street and town. That is stored with the check-in and syncs with it. You can remove the location from any check-in by editing it.

On shared actions: the people on an action you share can see the locations on its check-ins, just as they can see notes and photos. So your check-ins on shared actions only save a location if you turn on Show my location on shared actions in Settings → Location — it is off unless you do. If you add a location to a check-in yourself, they can see it either way, and the app tells you so as you add it.

Location names and maps come from Apple Maps. To name a location, its coordinates are sent to Apple; to draw a map, Apple is sent the area being shown. Apple handles this under its own privacy policy. I never see any of it.

Sharing and collaboration

You can invite someone to collaborate on an individual action. When you do:

  • They can see that action only — its check-ins, notes, photos and any locations attached to them.
  • They cannot see any of your other actions.
  • They can add their own check-ins to it. Only the person who wrote a check-in can edit it; the owner of the action can also delete anyone’s, so a shared action can be tidied up.
  • You can stop sharing at any time from the action’s Share tab.

Invitations are sent as a link — through Messages, Mail or whichever method you choose. Only the person you invite can accept it. Someone who obtained the link another way cannot use it to reach your data.

What other people can see

Only the people you invite, and only on the actions you invite them to.

Your nickname and photo travel with you onto an action when you share it, or when you accept someone’s invitation — so they can tell who wrote which check-in. They are copied into that action’s storage in iCloud. A copy is also kept in your own iCloud, so they come back if you reinstall Retainer or move to a new iPhone. They go nowhere else. Someone you have never shared with has no way to look you up, because there is nothing to look up:

  • There is no public directory of Retainer users.
  • There is no search for people by name or username.
  • Nothing about you is written anywhere other users can browse.

The action library

Retainer comes with a library of ready-made actions you can start from. The app fetches that library from a public area of Retainer’s iCloud storage, so entries can be corrected or added without waiting for an app update.

This is a one-way read. Nothing about you and nothing from your journal is sent when it happens, and the library works from a copy stored in the app if the fetch fails or you are offline.

What I do not do

  • I do not track you, across this app or anywhere else.
  • I do not use advertising, or share anything with advertisers.
  • I do not use analytics services, crash reporters or any third-party SDKs that collect data.
  • I do not sell, rent or trade your information. I could not if I wanted to, because I do not hold it.
  • I do not build profiles of you. The only decision the app makes about you on its own is the age check described under Children, and it is made on your iPhone.

Diagnostics

If you choose to send Apple diagnostic and usage data in iOS Settings, Apple may share aggregate reports with me about crashes and performance — for example how long the app takes to open. These contain no personal information and cannot be linked to you.

If you email me

If you write to retainerapp@icloud.com, I keep your email address and what you wrote, so I can reply and deal with it. I need to do this to answer you, and to meet my legal duties to keep the app safe. I delete the emails once they are no longer needed, and always within two years.

For problems and complaints about how Retainer is used, I also keep a short note: the date, the kind of problem and what I advised. It has no name or email address in it, and I use it only to spot patterns and keep the app safe.

Deleting your data

  • A single check-in or action — delete it in the app, and it is removed from your devices and from iCloud.
  • Everything — deleting the app removes the local copy. To remove the iCloud copy as well, go to Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → Retainer, and delete the data there.
  • Your nickname and photo — clear them in the app’s profile screen. The change is published to the actions you share, replacing what was there, and to the copy in your own iCloud. Leaving a shared action removes your copy from it entirely.

Because your journal is in your own iCloud account, deleting it there deletes it properly. I hold no backup copies of it.

Your rights

Data protection law gives you rights over your personal data, such as seeing it, correcting it and having it deleted. Your journal is in your own Apple account, so you can see, change or delete it yourself, in the app and in iOS Settings. I cannot see it or change it.

The only personal data I hold is what you send me by email. To ask about that, write to retainerapp@icloud.com and I will reply within a month. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office.

Children

Retainer is for adults aged 18 or over. It is not directed at children, and I do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 18.

In some places, such as Texas, the law requires apps to check each person’s age with Apple. There, Retainer asks Apple whether you are 18 or over, and should not open if you are not. Apple’s answer stays on your iPhone and is not sent to me.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is collected or who can see it, I will update this page and change the date at the top. Continuing to use the app after a change means you accept it.

Contact

Questions about privacy, or anything in this policy: retainerapp@icloud.com.
