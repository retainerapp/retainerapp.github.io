How long since?

Most trackers want you to build a streak, then make you feel bad for breaking it. Retainer answers a plainer question: when did I last do this? For the jobs that come round again, that is the only number that matters.

What it is for

The things that don’t suit a streak

Some things you want to do more often. Some you want to do less. And some you just need to know the date of. Retainer treats all three the same way, because the honest answer is always a date.

Every few months

Filters, descaling, the smoke alarm, the car. Things with no rhythm you could ever hold in your head.

Every day, ideally

Wall sits, the back exercises, ringing your mum. A gentle nudge, and a record that survives missing a day.

Less, please

The things you are cutting down on. Counting them is useful. Being congratulated for them is not.

Sharing

One action, not your whole journal

Share a single action with someone and they see that action only — its check-ins, notes and photos. Nothing else of yours is visible to them, and that is enforced by Apple’s servers rather than decided by the app.

Both of you can check in. Both of you see who did what, and when. It works well for anything a household shares: the filter that neither of you can remember changing, the plant that dies every summer.

Everything else

The rest of it, briefly

Two taps to check in

Add a note or a photo if you want one. Skip it if you don’t.

Reminders that make sense

Daily and weekly, or a set time after your last check-in — which is what a three-month filter actually needs.

Photos and places

Attach a picture to a check-in, and the place you were if you choose to. Location is always optional and always asked for.

A year at a glance

Every check-in on one grid, so a gap is obvious without reading a single number.

Nine looks

From plain iOS to newsprint. Typography and colour change together, not just the accent.

A starting library

Over a hundred ready-made actions if you’d rather not start at a blank screen.

Privacy

There is no account, because there is no server

Your check-ins live on your iPhone and in your own iCloud. Not on a server of mine — I don’t run one. I can’t read your journal, and neither can anybody else you haven’t invited to a specific action.

No advertising. No analytics. No third-party SDKs collecting anything. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to delete except the app itself.
